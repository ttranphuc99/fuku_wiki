High-Availability Transaction Processor

// handle downstream bank / partner outages gracefully

// 1 — the core problem

You don't control your dependencies

A transaction processor calls banks, card networks, and partners you have no control over. They will be slow or down. The design goal: a single partner's outage must not take down the processor or the partners that are healthy. Availability of your system ≠ availability of theirs — decouple the two.

Anchor scenario: Bank A is timing out (30s instead of 200ms). Without protection → threads pile up → pool exhausted → even Bank B and C traffic fails. One outage becomes total.
// 2 — containment: bulkheads + circuit breakers

Bulkhead — isolate resources per partner: separate thread pools / connection pools / concurrency limits. Bank A's slowness consumes only Bank A's bulkhead; B and C keep flowing. (The name is from ship compartments — one flooded compartment doesn't sink the ship.)

Circuit breaker — per partner, track the failure rate:

CLOSEDnormal; count failures
OPENthreshold breached → fail fast for a cooldown, no calls made
HALF-OPENafter cooldown, let a probe through; success closes, failure re-opens

Timeouts under everything — an unbounded wait is what fills the bulkhead in the first place. Set aggressive, per-dependency timeouts.

// 3 — graceful degradation, not failure
  • Store-and-forward: when a partner is down, accept the request, persist it durably, and queue for retry with exponential backoff — process when the breaker closes. (Only valid where async settlement is acceptable.)
  • Failover / alternate routing: if multiple acquirers can process the same payment, route around the failed one.
  • Dead-letter queue for messages that keep failing → human/ops attention, not infinite retries.
  • Backpressure & load shedding: bounded queues; reject or degrade cleanly rather than collapse under overload.

Every retry path assumes idempotency — replaying a queued transaction must not double-charge.

// 4 — HA of your own stack
  • No single point of failure: multiple stateless app instances behind a load balancer, multi-AZ.
  • Data HA: replicated DB with automated failover; understand the RPO/RTO you're promising.
  • Health checks + auto-restart (K8s liveness/readiness); readiness gates traffic during warm-up.
  • Active-active vs active-passive across regions — state the trade-off (complexity/consistency vs faster failover).
// 5 — what to monitor
  • Per-partner success rate, latency, circuit-breaker state transitions
  • Bulkhead saturation / rejected calls
  • Retry queue depth & age; DLQ depth
  • End-to-end transaction success rate (the customer-facing SLO)

⚡ Interview Talking Points

01
Lead with the cascade: "The failure I'm defending against is one slow partner exhausting a shared thread pool and taking down traffic to healthy partners. Bulkheads isolate that; the circuit breaker sheds it."
02
Decouple availabilities: "My system's availability shouldn't equal my worst partner's. Store-and-forward with a retry queue lets me accept the payment now and settle when they recover."
03
Always attach idempotency to retries: "Everything I retry has to be idempotent, or graceful degradation becomes graceful double-charging."
04
Show you know your own SPOFs too: "HA isn't just downstream — stateless multi-AZ instances, replicated storage with defined RPO/RTO, and readiness probes so I don't route to a cold instance."
